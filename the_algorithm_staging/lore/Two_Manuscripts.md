# Two Manuscripts

*A reading of "The Algorithm," placed beside the policy nobody wrote.*

The skill presents itself as a compression protocol. That is its cover story. What it actually does is re-price speech.

Fluency used to be evidence. If a memo was well-constructed, someone had constructed it, and construction cost attention, and attention was the tell that a mind had passed through. The engine broke that pricing the way counterfeit breaks currency — not by making bad money, but by making good money meaningless. The Algorithm's response is not to detect counterfeits. It is to reintroduce cost at four specific points and make the receipts mandatory.

**The fixed strings are performatives.** "Through the gate. Running." is a sentence that does what it says — the rarest object in modern paperwork. Everything in the admin genre is phatic: it maintains the channel, signals warmth, performs institution. The gate phrase is the opposite; it changes the state of the world, and it cannot be padded, because padding it breaks the string, and breaking the string is detectable by anyone. A liturgy is just a checksum you can hear.

**STE100 is a vocabulary too small to hide in.** Twenty words a line, one meaning per word, active voice. This is not about clarity for its own sake. It is that upholstery needs room, and the controlled vocabulary removes the room. Run the concluding-five-courses email through it and watch: eleven sentences of gratitude evaporate, three lines of withdrawal remain, and the residue is honest because the format cannot hold anything else. The floor test is an assay. What survives it was load-bearing. What evaporates was upholstery around a small hard thing.

**The Cut line is confession, required even when empty.** "Cut: nothing." is the reward state — which means the protocol treats *editing that admits itself* as the norm and silent revision as the deviation. Every institutional document you have ever received inverts this. The drift never announces its direction; it just flows toward the smooth. A mandatory Cut line is a drift meter bolted to the prose.

**The four nouns are the epitope.** Audience, Scope, Format, Path. Small enough to teach in a hallway, cheap enough to run against any incoming memo in thirty seconds, and — this is the part that matters — they work *reader-side*. Writer-side discipline does not spread; it costs the writer and benefits others, which is why it stays rare. Reader-side tests spread like jokes, because they cost nothing and confer sight. Yahoo's immunity was never a policy. It was that everyone could recognize "how is babby formed" by shape alone. The four nouns are that recognition, formalized.

One warning, and the story next door tells it better than I can: **a ritual survives its god.** The gate phrase can be delegated, and a delegate-model speaks it flawlessly, and the checksum still checks. The protocol's true invariant was never the string. It was that a human bore the cost of saying it, knowing what it was. Freeze that, or freeze nothing.

---

Now the juxtaposition, which is the only argument this document will make.

A year ago, the institution wrote an AI policy for students. It is, at its core, the novice rule: you may use the engine, and you must then sit with the output beside what you actually know, line against line, before you submit. It was written because the institution worried about the students. It applies to the people with the least authority and the most oversight.

The Algorithm is the same rule — elicit, cut, gate, verify, confess the edits — written by one faculty member, adopted by one faculty member, enforced on the only writer he had authority over.

The faculty policy is not missing. It exists. It has been in production for some time. Its sample size is one.

Both manuscripts are on the desk.
