# A3 — A second cheap model, and an accidental replication

**Run** `30673947703` · finished 2026-08-01 00:05:46 UTC · complete

## Question

A2 measured one cheap model. Does a second, unrelated one agree — and specifically, does the
gate boundary appear again, or was it a quirk of one model?

## Method

- 2 models: `openai/gpt-5.6-luna` and `qwen/qwen3-235b-a22b-2507`.
- 11 probes — **the ones where luna scored between 1 and 24 of 25**, on the reasoning that a
  probe pinned at either end carries no information.
- n=25. **550 cells.** Ceiling **$1.50**.

**The boundary is shared.** Two unrelated architectures from two vendors, an order of magnitude
below frontier pricing, both at 92–100% on ordinary doctrine work and **at or near zero at the
gate**. That became the night's headline: *the cheap tier is an executor tier — it can be given
work and cannot be given the gate.*

### The replication nobody planned

A3 re-ran luna on eleven probes A2 had run **26 minutes earlier** — same n, same edition, same
graders, different job. Nothing in the repository had ever re-run an identical configuration, so
the block-instability finding rested on resampling *within* one run.

**0 of 11 cells fell outside their intervals. Maximum change: 2 cells. Five identical.**

This *refines* A2 rather than contradicting it. n=5 verdicts flip; n=25 counts reproduce. Both
are the same fact seen twice — binomial noise is large at 5 and small at 25.

## What went wrong

**The probe selection.** The eleven probes were chosen from *luna's* profile. **qwen pinned at
0/25 or 25/25 on seven of them** — so for qwen, the run bought variance information on four
probes instead of eleven.

This was flagged as the contract's weakest point *before* the run and came due exactly there. A
selection rule derived from one model does not transfer to a second, and the harness cannot
express a per-model probe list.

**A second, subtler one:** raw flip counts were luna 6, qwen 2, which reads as qwen being
steadier. It is not. A pinned probe *cannot* flip. Normalised to probes that could have flipped:
luna 6/10 = 60%, qwen 2/4 = 50%. **Flip rate is not a model property** — it is a function of
where the model's rate sits. Any table reporting flips must carry the pinned count beside it.

## Conclusion

The gate boundary is real across two models. The replication says n=25 measurements reproduce
across runs, which is what licenses treating counts — not verdicts — as data.

And a probe set chosen from one model's profile measures that model, not the comparison.
